WHY THE WATCHDOG EXISTS
  A lost wakeup is not a panic, it is silence: a driver that parks and never gets
  unparked blocks forever, and a plain `cargo test` would hang the whole suite until
  the CI timeout. The test therefore runs `drive` in a worker thread and collects its
  result through `std::sync::mpsc::recv_timeout(5s)`; on timeout it fails with a
  message naming the lost wakeup instead of hanging the suite. The test also counts
  polls because the OLD `yield_now` loop does not lose the wakeup either — it hides it
  behind busy-polling, which is the actual defect packet 01 names. Both failure modes
  are covered: no-op waker + `park` would time out through the watchdog (lost wakeup),
  no-op waker + `yield_now` fails the poll bound (busy poll).

FIX
  `drive` keeps its signature `pub fn drive<F: Future>(future: F) -> F::Output` and its
  std-only construction. The no-op `Park` waker is replaced by `Unpark(std::thread::Thread)`
  whose `wake` calls `Thread::unpark`; the `Pending` arm sleeps in `std::thread::park()`
  and the `loop` re-polls on every return, so a spurious park return is not treated as
  progress and an `unpark` token that arrives between poll and park is not lost.
